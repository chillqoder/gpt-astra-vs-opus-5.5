Create a single, highly detailed 3D model of a classic Japanese street vending machine (jidōhanbaiki) using Three.js. This is NOT a scene or a street environment. It is one complete, showpiece-quality object, displayed alone against a simple dark neutral backdrop with soft studio-style lighting and a subtle contact shadow beneath it. Everything must be built from scratch in code, with no imported models and no downloaded image files, and the result should feel like a real, tangible object rather than a rough prototype.

OVERALL LOOK AND PROPORTIONS
The machine is a tall, slightly imposing rectangular cabinet, roughly 180 cm tall, 100 cm wide and 70 cm deep, with realistic proportions and gently rounded edges. Its color scheme is a glossy candy-red body with white and pale-grey accents, in the spirit of the drink machines found on every Japanese street corner. It stands on a low dark base plinth with small adjustable feet. The mood is nostalgic, clean and friendly, with a faint sense of everyday use: tiny scuffs, a few slightly faded stickers, subtle wear around the areas people touch most.

FRONT DISPLAY WINDOW
The upper two-thirds of the front is a large glass display window, framed by a thick glossy frame. Behind the glass, three or four shelves hold a rich variety of 16 to 20 different drinks in neat rows, each drink type repeated several times so the shelves look full and stocked. Include a believable mix: small canned coffees in different colors, green tea and barley tea in tall plastic bottles, a sports drink, a citrus soda, a milky yogurt-style drink, a cola, a carbonated marble-bottle soda, a peach juice, a lemon tea, a cocoa, a sparkling water, an energy drink and a couple of seasonal limited editions. Use only invented brands and playful made-up labels with simple graphics, colored caps, and short Japanese and English text. Cans and bottles should have distinct silhouettes, believable proportions, glossy or matte finishes, and slightly different heights. Each drink should be clearly readable and instantly recognizable as a separate product.

HOT AND COLD
Since Japanese machines serve both hot and cold drinks, mark the two zones clearly. Cold items get little blue labels (つめた〜い) and hot items get little red labels (あったか〜い). The hot canned drinks have subtle warm-toned details.

PRICE TAGS AND SELECTION BUTTONS
Under every drink sits a small price tag in yen (for example ¥130, ¥150, ¥160, ¥180). Directly beneath each price tag is a square selection button with a soft glowing edge. Available drinks have a gentle white or green glow. A few sold-out items show a red 売切 (sold out) light instead. The buttons should feel slightly pressable, with a visible bezel and a tiny gap around each one.

CONTROL PANEL
On the right-hand side is a vertical control column containing, from top to bottom:
- a small digital display showing the entered amount in glowing red or orange segment-style digits, with an occasional friendly message such as いらっしゃいませ (welcome);
- a coin slot with a bright rim and a small return-lever;
- a bill acceptor with a wide horizontal mouth, a glowing arrow light guiding the user where to insert money, and a subtle metallic frame;
- a round coin-return button and a small change-return cup;
- a contactless payment pad with a tiny wave icon that softly pulses;
- a small instruction sticker with pictograms and a few lines of Japanese text.

DISPENSING AREA
At the bottom front is a dark recessed pickup bay covered by a spring-loaded flap door with a soft light inside. Around it are a slightly worn rubber edge, a reflective inner chamber and a small label saying 取り出し口 (pickup opening).

TOP SIGN AND GLOW
The top of the machine carries a backlit header panel with a bold logo and slogan, and the whole machine emits its own light. The display window is lit from inside with a clean cold-white glow that gently reveals every drink, while warm accents and the buttons glow in different colors. The light should slowly flicker or breathe very slightly, like a real machine humming in the night.

SMALL DETAILS
Make the machine feel truly manufactured, with every surface carefully designed:
- visible seams and panel gaps between door sections, a vertical door line and a horizontal split;
- rows of screws and rivets around the frame, the control panel and the header;
- ventilation grilles with fine slots on the lower sides and back, plus a larger compressor grille;
- a keyhole lock with a small metal escutcheon on the door;
- hinges, small rubber bumpers, warning stickers, an energy-rating label and a serial-number plate;
- a small mascot sticker, a recycling notice and a thin decorative stripe running around the body;
- a cable entering the back and a tiny power indicator light.

MATERIALS
Use clearly different, convincing materials: glossy painted metal for the body, brushed and polished steel for hardware and frames, tough matte plastic for buttons and panels, translucent plastic for light covers, clear glass with subtle reflections and a slight tint for the display window, rubber for seals, and shiny aluminum and paper-like labels for cans and bottles. Reflections and highlights should make each material easy to tell apart.

INTERACTION
The viewer can freely rotate around the machine with smooth, inertial mouse or touch control, and zoom in and out within sensible limits, so every side can be inspected: the front, the sides, the ventilation grilles at the back, and the underside. The camera should never go below the floor or too far inside the model. Optionally, the model starts with a slow, gentle auto-rotation until the user interacts. The scene should run smoothly on ordinary laptops and phones and resize correctly with the window.

GOAL
The finished model should look like a lovingly crafted digital collectible: instantly recognizable as a Japanese vending machine, packed with charming small details, glowing warmly and coolly in the dark, and rewarding close inspection from every angle.

BUILD PLAN (follow these steps in order)

1. Set up the stage: create the Three.js viewer with a dark neutral backdrop, soft studio lighting, a floor with a subtle contact shadow, and smooth orbit-style camera control with sensible limits.

2. Build the main cabinet: model the red body with rounded edges, the base plinth with feet, the door split, panel gaps and the header box, using correct real-world proportions.

3. Add the display window: frame it, place the tinted glass in front, and build the interior with shelves, a back wall and internal lighting.

4. Fill the shelves: design each of the 16-20 drinks as a distinct can or bottle with its own shape, color, cap and label, then arrange them in neat repeating rows.

5. Add price tags and selection buttons: place a yen price and a glowing button under every drink, and mark a few items as sold out.

6. Build the control panel: add the digital display, coin slot, bill acceptor, coin return, contactless pad and instruction sticker.

7. Build the dispensing area: add the recessed pickup bay, spring flap, inner light and label.

8. Add the small details: screws, rivets, seams, ventilation grilles, lock, hinges, stickers, serial plate and power cable.

9. Set up materials and lighting: give every surface its own material, tune the glowing parts (header, window, buttons, display), and add a very subtle breathing flicker.

10. Polish: check the model from all sides, fix any gaps or floating parts, make sure it runs smoothly on laptops and phones, and finish with a gentle auto-rotation that stops on user interaction.
